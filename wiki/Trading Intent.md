# Trading Intent

> [!note] Definition
> **Trading intent** is the regulatory criterion that determines whether a financial position belongs in the [[Banking Book vs Trading Book|trading book]]. A position is held with trading intent if it is acquired for short-term resale, to benefit from price movements, or to hedge other trading positions.

---

## 1. Basel Definition (RBC25)

Under [[Basel RBC25]], trading intent includes positions that are:

1. Held for **short-term resale**
2. Intended to benefit from **actual or expected short-term price differences**
3. Used to **lock in arbitrage profits**
4. Held to **hedge risks** arising from other trading book positions

## 2. CRR Definition (Article 4(1)(85))

The [[EBA CRR — Trading and Banking Book|CRR]] defines "positions held with trading intent" as:

> **(a)** proprietary positions and positions arising from **client servicing and market making**
> **(b)** positions intended to be **resold short term**
> **(c)** positions intended to benefit from **actual or expected short-term price differences** between buying and selling prices or from other price or interest rate variations

## 3. Practical Implications

- Banks must document trading intent **at inception** of every position
- Intent cannot be changed retroactively to achieve a more favourable capital treatment
- Supervisors may challenge the classification if evidence of trading intent is weak
- Regular **marking-to-market** is required as proof of active management

> [!warning] Burden of Proof
> If a bank cannot demonstrate clear trading intent, the position defaults to the **banking book**, which may carry higher credit risk charges but avoids market risk capital.
