# Basel vs EBA — Boundary Comparison

> [!note] Purpose
> This page provides a comprehensive side-by-side comparison of how the Basel Framework ([[Basel RBC25|RBC25]]) and the EU's [[EBA CRR — Trading and Banking Book|CRR]] define and manage the boundary between the banking book and the trading book.

---

## 1. Definition Comparison

| Aspect | Basel Framework (RBC25) | EBA CRR Framework |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition Source** | RBC25.1 to RBC25.3 | CRR Articles 4(1)(85-86), 102, 103, 104b |
| **[[Trading Intent]]** | Short-term intent or hedging short-term risks | Adds specific governance around trading desk definition and internal models (Art. 104b) |
| **Governance** | Clear separation, less prescriptive | Granular: trading desk structures, trading limits, supervisory reporting (Art. 104b) |
| **[[Structural FX Positions]]** | Exclusion allowed if criteria met (RBC 25.5) | Similar exclusions tied to trading desks (Art. 104b(4)) |
| **Netting and Offsetting** | Allowed if quick repatriation feasible (RBC 25.6) | Flexibility for consolidated groups (Art. 103) with restrictions |

## 2. Article-Level Reference Mapping

| Aspect | Basel RBC25 | EBA CRR |
|---|---|---|
| Trading Book Definition | RBC 25.1–25.7 | Articles 104, 104b(1-2) |
| Banking Book Definition | RBC 25.7–25.10 | Articles 104a, 104b(3) |
| Boundary Management | RBC 25.7–25.10 | Articles 104, 104b |
| Flexibility for Transfers | Stringent (RBC 25.9) | More flexible (104b(3)) |

## 3. Key Divergences

### 3.1 Operational Complexity
- **EBA** emphasises a **phased approach** and reduced operational burdens, reflecting unique EU conditions (e.g., proportionality for small banks)
- **Basel** provides stricter **global consistency** with fewer regional exceptions

### 3.2 [[Internal Risk Transfers]]
- **EBA** permits more flexibility in internal transfers to manage book boundaries without unduly penalising banks
- **Basel** focuses on stricter controls to prevent misuse

### 3.3 Implementation Timeline
- **EBA** offers a **deferred and simplified** implementation framework for certain elements, recognising operational challenges within the EU
- **Basel** expects uniform global adoption

### 3.4 Sovereign Exposures
- **Basel**: no special treatment
- **EBA CRR Article 104a**: specific exemptions for sovereign and promotional exposures in the banking book

> [!important] Practical Impact
> These divergences mean that a bank operating in the EU may face **different capital requirements** than one in a jurisdiction that implemented Basel faithfully (e.g., Singapore, Hong Kong). This is a key consideration for internationally active banking groups.
