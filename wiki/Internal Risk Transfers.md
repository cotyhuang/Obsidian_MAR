# Internal Risk Transfers

> [!note] Definition
> An **internal risk transfer (IRT)** is a transaction between a bank's trading book and banking book that moves risk from one to the other without involving an external counterparty. These are heavily regulated to prevent capital arbitrage.

---

## 1. Why It Matters

Banks often wish to transfer risk internally — for example, a credit desk in the [[Trading Book vs Banking Book Boundary]] might hedge a loan portfolio in the banking book. Without strict controls, banks could:

- Reduce capital by routing positions through the more favourable book
- Obscure the true risk profile of each book
- Undermine the [[Trading Book vs Banking Book Boundary]]

## 2. Basel Approach

Under [[Trading Book vs Banking Book Boundary]]:

- IRTs are subject to **strict supervisory controls**
- The trading book leg must be hedged with an **external third party** for the capital benefit to be recognised
- Documentation requirements are extensive
- Focus on preventing misuse and ensuring **genuine risk transfer**

## 3. EBA CRR Approach

Under [[Trading Book vs Banking Book Boundary]]:

- More **flexibility** is afforded to EU banks for managing book boundaries
- IRTs are recognised without mandatory external hedging in some cases
- Emphasis on **proportionality** — smaller banks face lighter requirements
- Must still demonstrate that transfers are not motivated by capital reduction

## 4. Key Differences

| Aspect | Basel | EBA CRR |
|---|---|---|
| External hedge required | Yes (generally) | Not always |
| Documentation | Extensive | Extensive but proportional |
| Supervisory approval | Required | Required |
| Flexibility | Low | Higher |
