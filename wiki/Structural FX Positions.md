# Structural FX Positions

> [!note] Definition
> **Structural FX positions** are foreign-exchange exposures that arise from a bank's structural operations — such as investments in foreign subsidiaries or branches — rather than from active trading. These positions protect the bank's capital ratio against exchange-rate fluctuations.

---

## 1. Basel Treatment (RBC 25.5)

Under [[Basel RBC25]], structural FX positions may be **excluded** from market risk capital charges if:

- They are deliberately held to **hedge the bank's capital adequacy ratio** against FX movements
- They are of a **lasting structural nature** (e.g., capital invested in a foreign subsidiary)
- The supervisor approves the exclusion

> [!important] Supervisory Discretion
> The decision to allow exclusion rests with the national supervisor. Banks must demonstrate that the position genuinely hedges structural exposure, not speculative FX risk.

## 2. CRR Treatment (Article 104b(4))

Under [[EBA CRR — Trading and Banking Book|CRR]], the treatment is similar but adds:

- Structural FX positions are tied to **trading desk definitions** under Article 104b(4)
- More prescriptive governance requirements around identification and documentation
- Alignment with the broader [[FRTB Overview|FRTB]] boundary framework

## 3. Practical Example

A UK-headquartered bank with a EUR-denominated subsidiary might hold a EUR/GBP position to protect its consolidated capital ratio. If the EUR depreciates:

- The subsidiary's value in GBP terms falls → capital ratio pressure
- The structural FX hedge offsets this decline
- This hedge can be excluded from market risk capital if supervisory criteria are met
