The differences between the definitions of the banking book and trading book under the Basel RBC 25 framework and the European Banking Authority (EBA) CRR (Articles 104, 104a, and 104b) focus on regulatory boundaries, operational requirements, and treatment of instruments.

### 1.1.2 Basel RBC 25

1. **Trading Book**:
    
    - Comprises positions actively managed for short-term resale, benefiting from actual or expected short-term price differences, or hedging risks of other positions in the trading book.
    - Subject to specific market risk capital requirements.
2. **Banking Book**:
    
    - Includes positions held for long-term purposes, such as loans, investments, and liabilities managed through accrual accounting.
    - Exempt from market risk but subject to credit risk capital requirements.
3. **Boundary Management**:
    
    - Clear segregation of positions is emphasized with reclassification rules. Transfers between the books are tightly controlled to avoid regulatory arbitrage.

### 1.1.3 EBA CRR Articles 104, 104a, and 104b

1. **Trading Book**:
    
    - Defined similarly but includes more nuanced boundaries due to specific European exemptions and operational clarifications.
    - Allows for positions to be classified into the trading book if they meet the relevant liquidity and management intent criteria.
2. **Banking Book**:
    
    - Largely mirrors Basel definitions but incorporates specific regional considerations, such as sovereign exposures and promotional loans.
    - Includes detailed provisions for internal transfers and operational flexibility.
3. **Boundary Management**:
    
    - A significant focus on alignment with Basel while addressing regional concerns. Recent changes aim to align with the Fundamental Review of the Trading Book (FRTB) but adapt the timeline for EU implementation to address operational challenges.

### 1.1.4 Key Divergences

1. **Operational Complexity**:
    
    - EBA emphasizes a phased approach and reduced operational burdens, reflecting unique EU conditions (e.g., small banks).
    - Basel provides stricter global consistency with fewer regional exceptions.
2. **Internal Risk Transfers**:
    
    - EBA permits more flexibility in internal transfers to manage book boundaries effectively without unduly penalizing banks.
    - Basel focuses on stricter controls to prevent misuse.
3. **Implementation Timeline**:
    
    - EBA offers a deferred and simplified implementation framework for certain elements of Basel rules, recognizing operational challenges within the EU framework.

These differences reflect the EBA’s focus on proportionality and operational feasibility for EU-specific financial ecosystems while adhering to Basel principles where possible【74†source】【75†source】【76†source】.

## 1.2 **Basel RBC 25**

### 1.2.1 Trading Book

- **Definition**: Includes positions that are actively managed for short-term resale, benefiting from short-term price movements, or hedging other trading book risks.
- **Regulatory Requirements**: Subject to market risk capital requirements.
- **Boundary Management**:
    - Transfers between books are tightly controlled.
    - Classification depends on liquidity, management intent, and regular marking-to-market.

**Reference**: Basel Framework - RBC 25.1 to RBC 25.7

---

### 1.2.2 Banking Book

- **Definition**: Contains positions held for long-term purposes, such as loans, liabilities, and investments intended to be held to maturity.
- **Regulatory Requirements**: Exempt from market risk but subject to credit and operational risk requirements.
- **Boundary Management**:
    - Transfers allowed only under specific circumstances with supervisory approval.

**Reference**: Basel Framework - RBC 25.7 to RBC 25.10

---

## 1.3 **EBA CRR** (Articles 104, 104a, and 104b)

### 1.3.1 Trading Book

- **Definition**: Similar to Basel but tailored for EU-specific needs.
    - Includes all positions held for trading purposes or hedging risks within the trading book.
- **Regulatory Requirements**: Aligns with the Basel FRTB principles but incorporates European exemptions (e.g., promotional loans or sovereign exposures).
- **Boundary Management**:
    - Positions may be classified based on liquidity, frequency of valuation, and alignment with the institution's business model.

**Reference**:

- **CRR Article 104**: Defines the trading book and criteria for inclusion.
- **CRR Article 104b(1-2)**: Specifies liquidity and valuation criteria.

---

### 1.3.2 Banking Book

- **Definition**: Focuses on long-term exposures, loans, and liabilities not intended for active trading.
    - Includes sovereign exposures and exemptions for EU-specific instruments.
- **Regulatory Requirements**: Subject to credit risk capital requirements.
- **Boundary Management**:
    - Transfers allowed with detailed documentation and supervisory approval.

**Reference**:

- **CRR Article 104a**: Explains exclusions and the treatment of sovereign and promotional exposures in the banking book.
- **CRR Article 104b(3)**: Provides boundary rules for moving positions.
